## 0. Verdict vocabulary

| Word | Definition as used by this plan | Word | Definition as used by this plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **PASS** | every pre-registered criterion of the item holds as written, under the stated protocol | **FAIL** | a pre-registered criterion is not met; a control arm may fail by design (an informative failure) |
| **PARTIAL** | one clause of the criterion set holds and another does not (e.g. the wall holds, the interior optimum is not established) | **SUPPORT** | the positive direction of a pre-registered prediction is confirmed by the measurement |
| **MIXED** | some pre-registered criteria pass and some fail; the split is reported rather than averaged away | **REFUTED** | the measurement contradicts a pre-registered prediction; it stays on the record as-is |
| **UNDECIDABLE** | the criterion cannot be evaluated under the protocol as run (missing sweep level, no plateau, no localization) — a verdict is refused, not forced | **RESTRICTED** | the claim holds only inside a declared applicability domain (topology; task information sufficient to form a wall; cluster structure in the student's representation) |
| **USABLE** | fit for engineering use in the safe direction only — a necessary lower bound, never an exact value | **VERIFIED** | a toolkit function passed its hard criterion inside a declared task family |
| **CANDIDATE** | plausible but not verified; recorded as-is and not claimable | — | — |

Two qualifiers modify these without softening them: "**PASS but with weak evidence**" = the criterion passed while a stated counter-argument caps its power; "**closed out as an honest negative result**" = the item failed and the failure itself is the deliverable.

## 3. P1 | Tightness of the capacity upper bound log K

- **Content / protocol**: does the measured codebook entropy H(Z) of a discrete bottleneck (an unbiased proxy for I(X;Z)) grow logarithmically with K — is `log K` "tight" in practice (the network really fills the codebook) or "loose" (collapse)? Fix d=32, sweep K∈{4,8,16,32,64,128,256}, train each for 3 epochs, compute H(Z) and utilization on the test set.
- **Success / falsifier**: fitted slope over the last segment (K=128→256) **≥ 0.7**; falsified by slope **< 0.5 and utilization at the top two levels < 50%** (codebook collapse, loose upper bound — the toolkit is only worthwhile once a codebook-reset trick is added); middle ground 0.5–0.7, or a low slope together with high utilization → "partial pass". **Honest note**: H(Z) ≤ log K is a mathematical tautology; the non-trivial content is the **slope** — how tight the upper bound is, which directly determines the toolkit's practical value.
- **First run (2026-09-19, naive VQ, no reset) → failure**: H(Z) 0.69/1.05/1.68/1.73/1.75/1.93/2.05, utilization 50%/38%/38%/19%/9%/5%/3%, acc 0.198/0.249/0.539/0.549/0.524/0.602/0.666 for K=4/8/16/32/64/128/256; last-segment slope = 0.17 ≪ 0.7. **The root cause is the toolkit implementation, not the theory**: naive VQ has no codeword reset, so dead codes are never selected (at K=256 utilization is only 3%), H(Z) is locked at ~2 nats and the tightness of log K cannot be measured — exactly why the original VQ-VAE paper (van den Oord et al. 2017) requires dead-code restart. **Fix (2026-09-19)**: `VQBottleneck.update_usage_and_restart()` implements EMA usage counting + dead-code reset (relative threshold 0.1 — a codeword whose usage is below 10% of the average level is declared dead and reset to a random representation of the current batch), called automatically after every `optimizer.step()` in `train_one_epoch`; the P1 output gains a `restart=` count column. **Falsification boundary**: this round of failure **does not constitute a falsification of the `log K` upper bound** — it proves "under naive VQ the upper bound is not measurable"; only a failure persisting after the fix would falsify the toolkit's value.
- **Result after the fix**: utilization 100% at every K; H(Z)/logK = 0.91/0.74/0.92/0.91/0.91/0.93/0.93; acc = 0.327/0.414/0.539/0.764/0.810/0.870/0.884; **last-segment slope = 0.95** → ✅ **PASS** (before the fix the same levels had util 50%/38%/38%, slope 0.17 and acc(K=256)=0.666).

## 4. P2 | The min form of serial bottlenecks (wide vs deep)

- **Content / protocol**: at a comparable parameter count, is task accuracy locked dead by the **narrowest stage** when 1 wide bottleneck (K=256) is compared against 3 narrow bottlenecks in series (each K=6, total combinations 6³≈256)? Train both 3 epochs; measure the wide bottleneck's H(Z), the deep chain's last-stage H(Z3), the joint entropy H(Z1,Z2,Z3) and both accuracies.
- **Success / falsifier**: `acc_wide − acc_deep ≥ 0.10` **and** `H_wide > log 6` (the wide bottleneck really does use more states); falsified by `acc_wide − acc_deep < 0.05` (narrow serial accuracy is nearly unrestricted → `min` has no predictive power for task performance; first check whether the VQ has degenerated into an approximate identity). **Honest note**: H(Z3) ≤ log 6 is a mathematical tautology (Z3 takes only 6 values), so **that part of the "verification" is trivial**; two non-trivial contents remain — ① the magnitude of the task-accuracy locking effect; ② the joint entropy H(Z1,Z2,Z3) can approach log 216 and look like "capacity is the sum of the three stages", yet the end-to-end information is still pinned by min — exactly the intuitive error the ∑→min correction was meant to prevent, turned into a visible counterexample.
- **Result**: acc_wide=0.886 (33642 parameters) vs acc_deep=0.174 (28138); difference **0.713**; H_wide=5.00; H(Z3)=0.67 (≤ log 6=1.79); joint entropy H(Z1,Z2,Z3)=**1.74** (≤ log 216=5.38) → ✅ **PASS**.

## 5. P3 | Capacity matching accelerates convergence

- **Content / protocol**: convergence is fastest when the bottleneck width d matches the information the task requires; when d is too small it hits the capacity wall and converges slowly. Fix K=256 (so it is not a bottleneck), sweep d∈{2,4,8,16,32,64}, train each for at most 10 epochs, record the epoch at which 90% test accuracy is reached.
- **Success / falsifier**: the fastest d* is an **interior point**, and every configuration with d < d* is ≥ 1 epoch slower than d*; falsified if the convergence epoch is monotonically non-increasing in d and fastest at the smallest end (no capacity wall → "capacity matching" does not hold). **Honest note**: the slowdown at the large-d end (parameter waste / optimization noise) may not appear on MLP+MNIST, so it is **recorded as an observation only, not used as a hard criterion** — the weakened form of the prediction; the full form needs a harder task (v2). **Criterion encoding fix (2026-09-19)**: recording "did not reach 90%" as `None` with a "≥3 configurations converge" requirement left only d=32 and d=64 → "cannot be determined", throwing away the capacity-wall evidence; non-converged runs now take part under the natural encoding as **a +∞ lower bound (max_epochs+1)**, with the "number of wall-hit levels" reported in addition.
- **Measured lessons**: ① **fast budget trap (2026-09-19)** — the first `--fast` used "6 epochs × 30% subset" = 6×147 = **882** total steps ≪ the 4×469 = **1876** steps d=32 needs under the full budget, so no width converged and the criterion output a **false failure**; comparing "convergence speed" requires aligning the **total number of steps**, and it is now "full data + 8 epochs" (3752 steps). ② **criterion boundary bug (2026-09-19)** — `is_interior = any(d < best_d)` was **wrong**: any sweep entry smaller than d* declared an "interior point", so "**the largest d is fastest**" was misreported as "interior optimum" (d=2/8/32 did not converge, d=64 converged in 8 epochs, and the criterion printed "pass: the intermediate d* is fastest"). ③ **third-version fix** — interior-point definition `d_min < d* < d_max` with "partial/fail" at either end; **extracted into an independent function `p3_verdict(results, max_epochs)`** (independent of training) with 5 unit-test cases, one of which **directly reproduces the real misjudged input** and now returns "partial"; **multi-seed** with `seeds` initializations per d and the median taken (non-converged as +∞), each seed printed; plus "end-of-budget accuracy".
- **Reproducibility limitation / alternative form**: for the same d=32, full mode (10 ep) **converges in 4 epochs** while fast mode (8 ep, same data, same seed) **is not converged by >8**, because different `d_list` → different model creation order → a different random initialization: **the "convergence epoch" metric is sensitive to initialization and not reproducible across single runs**, and alone cannot support a fine-grained claim such as "interior optimum". The reliable alternative is **C2's "capacity ceiling" form** (accuracy under a fixed epoch budget) — on CIFAR the wall height is 0.372 and monotone.
- **Result**: **full** d=2/4/8/16 not converged by >10; d=32 → **4**; d=64 → **8**. **fast (8 ep, full data)** d=2/8/32 all not converged by >8; d=64 → **8** → ⚠️ **PARTIAL**: the capacity wall holds, **the interior optimum is not established**.

## 6. Feature A | Quantization lower bound q_min = 2^(R_IB/d)

- **Content / protocol**: does the per-dimension quantization level lower bound predicted by the formula correspond to the position of the post-training quantization accuracy cliff? Synthetic task (4×4×4 grid, 64 classes, d=3): ① train a VQ probe → **the Fano lower bound of R_IB = I(Z;Y)** (from the probe's error rate); ② train a continuous classifier → apply post-training **quantile-calibrated quantization** to the input (q levels per dimension), the sweep grid **dynamically including the levels at which q_min and q_min/2 lie**, and measure the accuracy at each level.
- **Success / falsifier**: the measured cliff `q_cliff` (the smallest level that keeps the baseline −0.02) falls inside **one octave** window [q_min/2, 2·q_min] of `q_min`, **and** the level at half of q_cliff really drops ≥ 5%; falsified by q_cliff > 2·q_min (too optimistic/too tight, underestimating the requirement), q_cliff < q_min/2 (too pessimistic/too loose), or the half level still keeping the baseline (no real capacity wall).
- **Which quantity to use for R_IB (2026-09-19 correction)**: ① **H(Z) cannot be used** — it grows freely up to log K and systematically overestimates the task information: measured H(Z)=5.36 nats **exceeds the task's own log 64 = 4.16 nats**, pushing q_min to 6.05 (the real cliff is at 4); ② **plug-in mutual information cannot be used either** — N=1920 ≪ K·C = 256×64 = 16384, so the plug-in is severely biased upward and the Miller–Madow correction presses it to **0.00**; the **Fano lower bound** is used instead (`task_info_fano`: only the error rate, no binning bias, conservative in direction) → R_IB ≥ 4.01 nats = 5.78 bits → q_min = 3.81; ③ the old criterion had a fatal bug — `ceil(q_min)` may **not lie inside the fixed sweep grid**, and `acc_by_q.get(q, 1.0)` supplied the default 1.0 → the criterion **necessarily passed**; now a dynamic grid + skipping the verdict when a level is missing.
- **Applicability domain and first-version lessons (fixed 2026-09-18)**: MNIST's information is only log 10 ≈ 3.3 bits, giving q_min = 2^(3.3/32) ≈ 1.07 at d=32 — the lower bound degenerates, so the formula is meaningful only for "small dimensions with a large amount of information" (**a declaration of the applicability domain**, not the concealment of a defect; the synthetic task puts the cliff at a non-trivial q=4 rather than q≈1). Lessons: ① 3 ReLU units span at most 8 linear regions and cannot distinguish 64 classes (acc stalls at 0.17) — fix: drop the hidden layer (Linear(3→64) implements nearest-centroid classification); ② min/max uniform quantization bin edges do not align with the clusters (edges fall near cluster center 6, and at q=4 classes 6/9 always collide) — fix: quantile-calibrated quantization (the TensorRT percentile calibration idea), edges in the valley floor; ③ cosine cannot distinguish collinear grid cluster centers — fix: a `metric="euclid"` option on VQBottleneck. **Honest note**: this quantizes the **input space** (the 3 dimensions the information must pass through), not a learned intermediate layer — a learned linear map would rotate the grid and confound "axis-aligned binning vs insufficient capacity"; PTQ of an intermediate layer needs QAT (v2+ scope).
- **Calibration artifact (second run, 2026-09-18)**: q=5/6 drop anomalously (acc≈0.45/0.55, below q=4's 0.98) — not insufficient capacity (125/216 cells ≥ 64 classes) but an **alignment artifact of quantile calibration**: 4 equal-mass modes per dimension, the quantile edges fall exactly in the mode valley floor when q is a multiple of 4 (4/8/16), and at q=5/6 the edges cut into a cluster's interior and split one class across levels. The PTQ cliff shape is jointly determined by "level count × calibration method"; the capacity lower bound only fixes the necessary direction. The toolkit should report this ("avoid naive quantile calibration at non-multiple level counts"); QAT (v2+) can remove it.
- **Multi-seed stability re-run (2026-09-19)**: protocol `--seeds 3` (full budget); each seed changes the **data realization** (`make_grid64(seed=s)`) and the **initialization/training randomness** (`torch.manual_seed(1000+s)`), the seed changing only the randomness, not the task's nominal information (still a 4×4×4 grid, 64 classes, σ=0.5, log 64 = 4.16 nats). **Aggregate criterion** (`ratio_verdict`, a pure function): the ratio measured cliff / predicted q_min — **median ratio inside [1/2, 2]** and **≥2/3 of the seeds inside the window individually** → pass; **precondition**: a run's cliff must be "sandwiched" (its half level really drops ≥5%), else that seed is not counted. **The window's limited discriminating power (exposed by a unit test)**: the cliff is **an integer and ≥2**, and with q_min ≈ 3.8 the lower bound 1.9 < 2, so **the lower half of the window is in practice unreachable** — the criterion reduces to the upper bound `cliff ≤ 7.6` doing any work, with almost no discriminating power on "the lower bound is too loose". This is the criterion's **real weakness**, not stated as a merit.
- **Results (2026-09-19, `--seeds 3`, full budget)**: seed 0 — probe acc 0.984 (util 99%), R_IB 4.01, q_min 3.81, measured cliff 4, ratio 1.05, pass; seed 1 — 0.984 (util 99%), 4.01, 3.81, 4, 1.05, pass; seed 2 — 0.994 (util 99%), 4.10, 3.92, 4, 1.02, pass. **Verdict ✅ PASS** — median ratio **1.05**, **3/3 seeds fall inside the window individually**. Full curves (q=2/3/4/5/6/8/12/16): seed 0: 0.118 / 0.302 / **0.983** / 0.466 / 0.546 / 0.983 / 0.983 / 0.981; seed 1: 0.139 / 0.305 / **0.962** / 0.473 / 0.564 / 0.962 / 0.962 / 0.961; seed 2: 0.124 / 0.285 / **0.977** / 0.466 / 0.549 / 0.977 / 0.977 / 0.977.
- **Most important finding and conclusion**: **the anomalous drops at q=5/6 reproduce in all three seeds** (0.466/0.546, 0.473/0.564, 0.466/0.549) → that "dirty cliff" is **not noise but a deterministic misalignment between quantile calibration and the four-mode cluster structure**, which the toolkit ought to report; the earlier judgment calling it "noise contamination" must be corrected to "systematic artifact". The cliff position (=4) and the prediction (3.81–3.92) are **stable and consistent** across the three seeds, differing by 0.02–0.05 of an octave: the single-run conclusion **is reproduced and is not luck** — but with the window's lower bound unreachable, what it verifies is "the prediction did not overestimate the requirement", not "the prediction is exact".

## 7. QAT | Quantization-aware training removes the PTQ calibration artifact (v2 item, 2026-09-19)

> Supporting commands: `python experiments/mnist_demo.py --only qat --seeds 3` (full, authoritative); `--fast` for a quick directional check.

- **Motivation / protocol** (`verify_qat`, three-arm comparison, same data realization, same seed convention): §6 found a systematic PTQ artifact at q=5/6 (reproduced across three seeds: acc 0.466–0.564, below q=4's 0.96+), and **the capacity lower bound only fixes the necessary direction (q≥q_min) while the cliff shape is jointly determined by "level count × calibration method"**; QAT's promise is to put quantization inside the training loop and remove it. ① probe (identical to feature A) → Fano lower bound of R_IB → q_min; ② **PTQ arm** (control): continuous classifier + post-hoc quantile quantization; ③ **QAT-uniform arm**: the **same set** of quantile edges and empirical-mean reconstruction values placed into the training loop as buffers (not learnable) — only the classifier adapts, and any artifact removed is attributed to "training adaptation"; ④ **QAT-vq arm**: one `VQ(1, q)` per dimension (learnable codeword positions + commit loss + dead-code reset, reusing `VQBottleneck`), codebook initialized from the same quantiles as PTQ — the difference from QAT-uniform is attributed to "quantization parameter learning"; ⑤ all three arms sweep the same q grid (including the levels where q_min and q_min/2 lie), full budget 60 epochs.
- **Criteria (pre-registered, `qat_verdict` pure function, unit-tested with 5 cases)**: **J1 artifact removal** — at every level q ≥ ceil(q_min), QAT acc ≥ continuous baseline − 0.02; **J2 capacity wall preserved** — the level q = ceil(q_min) − 1 is still ≥ 0.05 below the baseline (when d·log q < R_IB we have I(Z;Y) ≤ H(Z) = d·log q < R_IB, Fano forces an error: **a mathematical wall at the DPI level that QAT must not rescue**); pass = J1 ∧ J2, multi-seed ≥ 2/3 seeds pass.
- **Quick smoke run (2026-09-19, 25 ep × 50/class, probe only 0.798, q_min=2.56 — the numbers do not count but the mechanism signal is strong)**: QAT-vq rescues the artifact levels — q=5 from 0.352 → **0.756**, q=6 from 0.477 → **0.842** (recovery ~0.4); **QAT-uniform cannot rescue them** — q=5/6 = 0.359/0.469, almost the same as PTQ (0.352/0.477), because the split clusters are **linearly inseparable** in the fixed reconstruction-value space and the edges/codewords must move. **fast budget defect and its fix (2026-09-19)**: the first `verify_qat` set fast to the same budget as quick (copying `verify_quant`'s `quick or fast → 25 ep × 50/class`), so "fast" was really quick + a verdict and the 0.784 baseline under that low budget made the J1 verdict unfair; changed to a genuine intermediate level (40 ep × 100/class, consistent with `verify_distill_grid`).
- **fast results (2026-09-19, 40 ep × 100/class, seed 0; probe acc=0.984, R_IB=4.01, q_min=3.80, continuous baseline 0.953)**: q=2 → PTQ 0.125 / QAT-uniform 0.109 / QAT-vq 0.122; q=3 → 0.304 / 0.292 / 0.380 (wall level, d·log3=3.30 < R_IB=4.01); q=4 → 0.980 / 0.980 / 0.994 (safe level, all three arms agree); q=5 → 0.441 / 0.442 / **0.988** (artifact level); q=6 → 0.583 / 0.589 / **0.984** (artifact level); q=8 → 0.967 / 0.966 / 0.971; q=12 → 0.955 / 0.966 / 0.974; q=16 → 0.953 / 0.951 / 0.959. **Verdict: QAT-vq PASS** (J1: every q≥4 keeps the baseline −0.02, artifact levels recovering from 0.441/0.583 to 0.988/0.984, removal amounts **+0.55/+0.40**; J2: the q=3 drop is 0.573 ≥ 0.05, the DPI wall preserved); **QAT-uniform FAIL** (J1: q=5/6 = 0.442/0.589, still below the baseline −0.02; J2 passes).
- **full results (2026-09-19, 60 ep × 150/class, 3 seeds) — ✅ PASS**: probe acc 0.984/0.984/0.994, R_IB 4.01/4.01/4.10, q_min 3.81/3.81/3.92, continuous baseline acc 0.971/0.981/0.983 (seed 0/1/2). Three-arm acc (PTQ / QAT-uniform / QAT-vq, s0/s1/s2): q=2 0.118/0.139/0.124 · 0.119/0.128/0.121 · 0.114/0.121/0.125 (deep wall level); q=3 0.302/0.305/0.285 · 0.299/0.306/0.307 · 0.373/0.382/0.399 (wall level); q=4 0.983/0.962/0.977 · 0.983/0.963/0.977 · 0.990/0.993/0.995 (safe level); q=5 0.466/0.473/0.466 · 0.452/0.475/0.464 · **0.986/0.987/0.992** (artifact level); q=6 0.546/0.564/0.549 · 0.565/0.573/0.571 · **0.985/0.984/0.991** (artifact level); q=8 0.983/0.962/0.977 · 0.983/0.963/0.977 · 0.989/0.991/0.996; q=12 0.983/0.962/0.977 · 0.983/0.962/0.977 · 0.991/0.990/0.994; q=16 0.981/0.961/0.977 · 0.981/0.959/0.975 · 0.982/0.985/0.991. **Verdict (multi-seed aggregate, threshold ≥ 2/3)**: QAT-vq 3/3 seeds pass (J1∧J2) → **PASS**; QAT-uniform **0/3 seeds pass J1** (J2 alone passes 3/3) — as the mechanism control arm, "failing" is its function; artifact removal amount (average recovery over the safe levels where PTQ drops ≥0.05): QAT-vq mean **0.477** (range 0.467–0.484) vs QAT-uniform mean **0.006** (range 0.002–0.010), a gap of three orders of magnitude.
- **Mechanism finding (three-arm attribution)**: QAT-uniform and PTQ nearly coincide at **all 8 levels** (difference ≤ 0.006) — **"letting only the classifier adapt to a fixed quantization grid" contributes nothing to artifact removal**; removal comes entirely from quantization parameter learning (VQ codewords moved by the gradient + dead-code reset from their quantile initialization points to cluster-safe positions), because clusters split by a quantile edge are **linearly inseparable** under a fixed reconstruction grid. Engineering mapping: **QAT's gain lies in the quantization parameters (scale/codewords), not in weight adaptation** (on this task).
- **Consistency / reproducibility / honest note**: the PTQ artifact reproduces as-is under full (q=5/6 = 0.466–0.564 vs 0.96+ at q=4), ruling out "the artifact comes from insufficient training"; QAT-vq's recovery (mean +0.47) is of the same order as fast (+0.55/+0.40); two independent full runs produced **byte-for-byte identical** output (fully controlled seeds + deterministic CPU training). The levels on which QAT-uniform fails J1 for seed 1 additionally include q=16 (0.959, below the baseline 0.981−0.02), but PTQ at the same level is 0.961 (also 0.020 short) → a mild PTQ-level decay, not something QAT introduced; all conclusions rest on a synthetic task, and the QAT attribution on real datasets is untested.

## 8. Feature B | Distillation capacity lower bound K_min = ⌈e^R_IB⌉

- **Content and v2 protocol (MNIST)**: when the student codebook is ≥ K_min the student can carry the teacher's representation rate (the gap drops to an irreducible level), and below K_min the gap is markedly larger. Train the teacher (d=16, K=256) for 3 epochs → R_IB = H(Z_teacher) → K_min; the student sweeps **K∈{32, K_min−2, K_min, K_min+2, 192, 256}** (the top end 192/256 is necessary as a **plateau reference**), distillation objective = **CE + KL(T=3)**, 3 epochs each (**aligned with the teacher**).
- **v2 criterion (gap curve, redone as v2 on 2026-09-19)**: ① gap `g(K) = acc_teacher − acc_student(K)`; ② reference plateau `g_ref = g(K_max)` — the largest-K student is the least capacity-limited, so its gap is the **irreducible cost** (the KD objective + a finite budget); ③ `K*(gap) = min{ K : g(K) ≤ g_ref + tol }`, `tol = 0.02`; ④ success: `K*(gap)` within **one octave** of `K_min` (i.e. `K_min ∈ [K*/2, 2K*]`); ⑤ **precondition**: the plateau must already be established (`g(K_max) − g(K_second largest) ≤ tol`) — when it is not we **refuse to give K*** and record "not localized", otherwise a too-small g_ref pushes K* to the upper end of the grid, which then necessarily falls inside the octave and forms a **false pass**. **Falsifier**: `K_min > 2K*(gap)` (the lower bound is too conservative; the student built on that advice is overestimated) or `K_min < K*(gap)/2` (too loose; the student is in fact unusable), or a large fraction of seeds with no plateau / no localized K*.
- **Why the absolute threshold had to go**: the old criterion `acc_student ≥ acc_teacher − 0.03` — measured, the teacher rising from 0.864 to 0.884 (only 0.020 stronger) flipped K* from 132 to "no one on the whole grid qualifies": **the threshold is of the same order as the effect being measured**, so the verdict is decided by how well the teacher happened to train, while teacher strength is unrelated to "how much capacity the student needs"; with the largest-K student's gap **within the same run** as reference, `g(K) − g_ref` is independent of the teacher's absolute level (unit-tested: teacher strength 0.80 / 0.86 / 0.90 all give the same K*). **Why R_IB used H(Z_teacher) (v2)**: distillation transfers the teacher's **soft labels/representation structure**, whose information can exceed the class-label information H(Y) (only 2.3 nats for 10 classes); an early single run saw H(Z)=5.26 nats → K_min=192 agreeing with a turning point of 194, **but that agreement did not survive multi-seed testing**, so it is no longer used as supporting evidence.
- **Budget alignment / objective fixes (2026-09-19)**: the old protocol trained the teacher 3 epochs but the student only 2, so the student was systematically underfitted (short by 0.008 on MNIST and 0.066 on CIFAR), K* was pushed up and the criterion necessarily failed — a **configuration defect**, not a falsification of the formula; now `ep_student = ep_teacher`. Soft-label KL alone underfits with a weak teacher (MNIST 0.881 / CIFAR 0.498) → changed to the standard KD combination **CE + KL**.
- **v2 first round (old criterion, exposed a protocol defect)**: a single run gave K*=146 while **K=148 and 150 actually dropped** (0.832 / 0.712 / 0.752), and "predicted 148 vs measured 146, only 1.4% apart" is very likely a **coincidence driven by single-seed noise**. Protocol: `--seeds 3`; each seed changes the teacher initialization/training randomness (`torch.manual_seed(1000+s)`), while the student uses an independent seed tied to (seed, K) (`2000+100s+K`) to decorrelate; **the K grid is anchored to seed 0's K_min and shared by all seeds**; the aggregate uses the same `ratio_verdict` as feature A. Partial results (seed 0/1; seed 2 was not written to disk): seed 0 — teacher 0.864, K_min 134, qualifying line 0.834, measured K* **132**; seed 1 — teacher 0.884, K_min 134, line 0.854, K* **None (no student on the whole grid qualifies)**. **Conclusion (negative and important)**: a **0.020**-stronger teacher flipped K* from 132 to "does not exist" — the original claim **is not reproducible**, the root cause being a **protocol defect** (the qualifying line is of the same order as the student's systematic gap); feature B was therefore downgraded for a time to "undetermined" (the lowering of the original G3 comes from this).
- **v2 second round (gap-curve criterion; 2026-09-19, `--seeds 3`, 30% subset budget)**: K grid `[32, 132, 134, 136, 192, 256]` (anchored to seed 0's K_min=134). seed 0 — teacher 0.864, R_IB 4.89, K_min 134, g_ref 0.011, gaps g(32/132/134/136/192/256) = 0.167/0.027/0.051/0.038/0.032/0.011, plateau yes, **K*(gap)=132**, old-criterion K* 132, ratio 0.99; seed 1 — 0.884 / 4.89 / 134 / 0.031, gaps 0.270/0.051/0.054/0.046/0.044/0.031, yes, **K*=136**, old **None**, ratio 1.01; seed 2 — 0.860 / 5.07 / 159 / 0.015, gaps 0.177/0.065/0.120/0.177/0.030/0.015, yes, **K*=192**, old 192, ratio 1.21. Criterion output: median ratio **1.01**, 3/3 valid seeds, 100% in-window → **PASS** (the rework removed the earlier failure: seed 1's `g_ref` rose from 0.011 to 0.031 and the new criterion **cancels** it out); the script also prints the old absolute criterion's K* as a comparison column.
- **Why that "pass" cannot be read literally — three counter-arguments**: ① **the plateau was in fact never established** — in all three seeds `g(256)` **is the minimum over the whole grid** and `g(192)` is 0.015/0.013/0.016 above it (mean 0.035 vs 0.019), so `g_ref` is not the irreducible cost, and the plateau criterion (top two levels within ≤ tol) is **too weak** (0.021/0.013/0.016 all scraped past the line). ② **the neighborhood is dominated by training noise; seed 2's K*=192 is not a capacity wall** — its gap **increases monotonically** within the neighborhood (132→0.065, 134→**0.120**, 136→**0.177**, equal to the 0.177 at K=32), i.e. two badly trained students rather than insufficient capacity; the three seeds' fluctuations within 132–136 are 0.024/0.008/0.112, mean **0.048** = **2.4×** `tol=0.02` — **the criterion's resolution is finer than the noise**; mean gap curve 32:0.205 → 132:0.048 → 134:0.075 → 136:0.087 → 192:0.035 → 256:0.019 (non-monotone after 132). ③ **the grid has a large hole between 32 and 132**, so a true knee inside (32, 132) cannot be detected; the grid can only give 6 candidate ratios (0.24 / 0.99 / 1.00 / 1.01 / 1.43 / 1.91) — the octave test in substance only asks "does K* fall inside {132,134,136,192}", **its verifying power is very weak**.
- **Verdict of the v2 line**: `⚠️ PASS but with weak evidence` — compared with the first round's "undetermined" this is **a change from not-localizable to localizable but not resolvable**, not a change from failure to success. **Deeper conclusion (more important than the criterion itself)**: on this task line (MNIST + MLP + VQ student) **the dependence of accuracy on capacity is smooth** — the gap falls all the way from K=32 to K=256 with no inflection point; `⌈e^{R_IB}⌉` gives the **necessary capacity to carry the representation rate**, but "is the capacity enough" shows up in accuracy as something continuous, with **no sharp cliff to be read off** — an **operationalization failure** on this task, not a wrong formula. **Four optional fixes**: (a) N student initializations per (seed, K) and average, pressing the noise from ~0.05 down to ~0.05/√N; (b) complete the grid (48/64/96) so the knee becomes visible; (c) **abandon the cliff framework** and report "whether the difference between `g(K_min)` and the plateau gap is smaller than the noise level"; (d) **switch to a task with a hard information wall** (chosen): reuse `make_grid64` (64 classes, H(Y)=log 64=4.16 nats) + a VQ student — for K<64, `log K < H(Y)` and DPI+Fano **mathematically forbid** the student from keeping accuracy; **if it is still smooth under (d), feature B is removed from the claimable list entirely** (also a clean conclusion).
- **Criterion implementation bug: `distill_plateau_ok` had its sign reversed (found by a v3 unit test, fixed)**: it was implemented as `acc(second largest) − acc(largest) ≤ tol`, whereas its meaning is `g(second largest) − g(largest) ≤ tol`, equivalent to **`acc(largest) − acc(second largest) ≤ tol`** — the opposite direction. Consequence: when the top is still clearly rising (exactly the trap that should be refused) the implementation computed **a negative number ≤ tol, which always holds**, misjudged as "plateau established" — **what should have been blocked was let through**; conversely a largest-K student that happened to train badly was rejected. Re-checked against seed 0 (`g(192)=0.032`, `g(256)=0.011`, top difference **0.021 > tol=0.02**): semantically "plateau not established", but the log judged "yes" and let K*=132 through; seeds 1/2 (0.013/0.015 ≤ tol) are unaffected. Impact: the "3/3 localizable" is in fact **2/3** → `ratio_verdict`'s "must not silently exclude" rule gives at least "partial", and the second round is downgraded to **"partial (after fixing the criterion implementation bug)"**. Fix: `return (accs[ks[-1]] - accs[ks[-2]]) <= tol`; unit tests: top still rising → False (a regression for the old misjudgment direction), the measured MNIST seed 0 data → False, a normal curve in a hard-wall scenario → True.

## 9. P4 | Residual/bypass topology makes the serial `min` bound fail (v2's first item)

- **Content**: the applicability boundary of `min_capacity_serial` — it holds only for **pure serial chains without a bypass**; as soon as a parallel branch exists (a residual identity or a parallel bottleneck) the end-to-end capacity is no longer locked dead by the narrowest stage and `min_capacity_serial` gives deployment advice that is **wrong in direction**, which should instead come from `sum_capacity_parallel`.
- **Protocol** (MNIST, same budget, four topologies, **the bottleneck-chain part completely identical**: three stages of K=k_each each → serial min = log k_each):

  | arm | topology | rule prediction |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | (a) | `DeepVQNet`: pure serial 3×K=k_each | `min` = log 6 = 1.79 nats (predicts unchanged accuracy) |
  | (b) | (a) + **parallel discrete wide bottleneck** K=k_wide | misapplying `min` → 1.79 (unchanged, **wrong**); `sum` → log6 + log256 = 7.34 (a lift) |
  | (c) | (a) + **parallel continuous bypass** (residual identity) | same as (b); the bypass capacity is not given by log K (continuous limit) |
  | (d) | single wide bottleneck K=k_wide (reference) | log 256 = 5.55 — the level the wide branch alone can reach |

- **Success / falsifier**: lift = `acc(b) − acc(a) ≥ 0.30` **and** `acc(b) ≥ acc(d) − 0.10` (the parallel version reaches the single wide bottleneck → `min` fails and the additivity direction is correct); falsified by lift `< 0.10` (the parallel branch barely does anything → additivity does not hold and `min` still holds with a bypass present); middle ground: lift ≥ 0.30 but more than 0.10 below the single wide bottleneck → "partial: the upper bound given by additivity is too optimistic".
- **Honest notes (key)**: ① both branches of (b) **are discrete bottlenecks**, so "parallel only wins because there is an extra continuous path" does not apply to it — comparing (c)/(d) shows that (b)'s lift is not a free continuous channel; ② this proves "**`min` fails when a bypass is present and the direction should follow additivity**", **not** "the end-to-end capacity equals exactly the sum of the branches' log K" — a rigorous quantification needs an independent estimate of the branch capacities (a continuous bypass's effective dimension can be characterized by the Fisher effective dimension, v2 follow-up work); ③ `H(Z3) ≤ log 6` still holds identically in (b)(c): the bypass changes only the **end-to-end** achievable information, not "the information inside the bottleneck chain is pinned by the narrowest stage" — this is where "one must judge the topology before reporting an upper bound" comes from. **Relation to P2**: P2 is the **bypass-free** version of the same comparison (difference 0.713, `min` holds); P4 makes "bypass present or not" the only independent variable, so only the two together state the scope in which `min` applies.
- **Results (2026-09-19)**: acc **full** / fast — (a) 0.199 / 0.103, H(Z3)=1.34 (≤ log 6=1.79); (b) 0.886 / 0.876, H(Z3)=1.49; (c) 0.956 / 0.914, H(Z3)=1.71; (d) 0.886 / 0.863, H(Z)=5.00. **Verdict (full, authoritative)**: lift = **+0.688** ≥ 0.30; `acc(b) − acc(d) = −0.000` (**exactly equal**) → ✅ **PASS**. (b)'s and (a)'s chains are **architecturally identical** (both 3×K=6 → `min` = log 6 = 1.79 nats) with only (b)'s extra parallel wide-bottleneck branch differing, yet accuracy jumps from 0.199 to 0.886; the two rules make opposite predictions (`min` → unchanged 1.79 nats, `sum` → a lift 7.34 nats) and the measurement **sides with `sum`**, with (b) and the wide branch alone (d) **coinciding exactly**. **Rebuttal of "a free continuous channel"**: both branches of (b) **are discrete bottlenecks** (a VQ chain + a VQ wide branch) with no unquantized path, and (c) (a genuine continuous bypass) is only 0.070 above it. **Comparison with P2**: (a)=0.199 is of the same order as P2's deep arm 0.174 — **the only independent variable is the topology**; fast/full differences +0.096 / +0.010 / +0.042 / +0.023 with an identical order and verdict (under fast (a)'s chain collapsed to H(Z3)=0.00 while under full it recovers to 1.34 with accuracy still 0.199 — both on the "locked dead" side).
- **Honest limitations**: ① (c)'s bypass is an **unquantized continuous path** whose capacity cannot be given by log K; ② the second condition (`acc(b) ≥ acc(d) − 0.10`) amounts to "exactly equal" here and is not the decisive basis for the pass — the decisive basis is the first condition (lift +0.688).

## 11. Transformer line (T1–T2)

> Supporting commands: `python experiments/transformer_demo.py` (CPU; `--fast` reduces the budget but still gives a verdict). **Why this line is needed**: MNIST+MLP and CIFAR+CNN are both "feed-forward", whereas a Transformer block is **residual + parallel** (multi-head attention ‖ FFN, each with its own identity bypass) — exactly the topology class for which P4 established that the `min` bound fails, so the Transformer is **the real carrier of the P4 conclusion**.   | arm | structure | rule prediction |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | (a) | pre-LN residual blocks, a narrow VQ (K=k_ffn) inserted **inside the FFN branch of every layer** | the bottleneck is bypassed by the identity stream, `min` **does not apply** |

## 12. Fisher effective dimension (v1 module acceptance)

- **Content / protocol**: can the gradient Gram participation ratio d_eff serve as a usable training monitor? Train Net(d=32, K=256) for 3 epochs, collect ≤32 batch gradients per epoch, compute d_eff, and output the curve.
- **Engineering acceptance (hard criterion)**: all `0 < d_eff ≤ 32` (the number of cached batches), finite, non-zero, no NaN; re-running the same seed gives an identical curve. **Scientific observation (soft, not used as a criterion)**: if d_eff shrinks with training (the gradient subspace collapsing → entering a low-dimensional basin) it is recorded as a positive observation; not shrinking is not a failure either (the last layer of MLP+MNIST may always dominate the direction). **Result**: d_eff 4.45 → 2.95 → 2.30 (acc 0.708→0.735→0.813) → ✅ **PASS** (engineering acceptance), **shrinkage observed**; the CNN version (C4) does not shrink: 2.27 → 7.20 → 6.16.
